Australian university students, 2025

The University Experience

More than 3 in 4 undergraduates rate their university experience positively. Fewer than half feel they belong. This story looks at where universities meet students' expectations, and where the gaps are.

1

The big picture

Every year, the national Student Experience Survey asks a large sample of current students to rate their course. In this story, a positive rating means a student chose one of the top answers on the survey's scale, such as "good" or "excellent".

2

Where students study

Most Australians enrol with a university in their own state, so it helps to know how the local universities compare. The national results are not evenly spread across the map.

3

Universities and study areas

Nationally, learning resources (83.9%) and teaching (80.0%) earn the highest positive ratings from undergraduates. Peer engagement, meaning how much students connect with each other, earns the lowest (59.9%).

4

Everyday experience

The broad ratings hide the details of daily student life: getting help, meeting other students, and deciding whether to stay.

About the data

Sources

  • Student Experience Survey 2025, National Report Tables. Quality Indicators for Learning and Teaching (QILT), Social Research Centre for the Australian Government Department of Education, published 2026.
  • Higher Education Student Data 2024, Section 2: All students. Australian Government Department of Education, published September 2025.
  • State boundaries: Natural Earth 1:50m admin-1 (public domain), simplified.
  • Campus locations: approximate main-campus coordinates, compiled by the author.

Reading the numbers

  • Survey results are from 2025 and enrolment counts from 2024, the latest years available.
  • A "positive rating" is the share of respondents choosing the top answers for a question or group of questions.
  • "Points" means percentage points: 78% is 2 points above 76%.
  • The survey asks a sample of students, so each result has a margin of uncertainty. Small differences between universities may not be real.
